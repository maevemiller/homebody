# Capture: from real photos and video to a photorealistic 3D room

Research for Space Capture, 2026-10-27. The question: how does Homebody turn a person's real room photos or video
into a 3D model that looks like *their* room, while keeping furniture as separate objects for Keep / Move / Remove
and the Plug & Play editor?

**Today:** photos → Claude reads a to-scale spec (`lib/scan/analyze.ts`) → Blender 5.2.3 builds a parametric room
(`blender/build_room.py`): right sizes, generic Poly Haven CC0 materials tinted by Claude's colors, one outdoor
HDRI, furniture generated by category. It is a good stand-in, but it reads as "a room like yours", not "your room".

## 1. The options

Ratings are relative, for an interior with furniture. "Separable" means you get furniture as distinct objects
you can hide, move or swap, which Keep / Move / Remove and the editor require.

### Photogrammetry

**Apple Object Capture** (`PhotogrammetrySession`, RealityKit) ships with macOS 12+ and runs on this Mac
(`isSupported` returned true). It takes a folder of JPEG/HEIC/PNG images or a sequence of samples with optional
depth, gravity and masks. It produces USDZ at detail levels from `preview` to `raw`, plus a sparse point cloud and,
importantly for us, **per-image camera poses** (`.poses` request). Apple's guidance: 70%+ overlap between
neighbouring shots (under 50% may fail), locked exposure and focus, diffuse light, no zoom changes. With depth
data, models come out at real-world scale; without it you rescale (we already have a scale step).
WWDC24 added **area mode** for environments, not just objects. Apple notes that areas larger than about 6 ft lose
mesh and texture quality unless processed at higher detail on a Mac, and a developer forum thread reports macOS
sessions failing on some area-mode image sets.

- Realism: very good on textured furniture, rugs, wood floors and books. Weak on white walls, windows, mirrors,
  glossy or thin things. Rooms come out with holes and lumpy walls.
- Separation: none. The room is one mesh. **Per-item capture** (walk around one chair) works well and gives a
  real, separable model of the user's own piece.
- For Homebody: best used (a) on single furniture items, and (b) to recover **camera poses** for the room photos,
  so we can project the photos onto our clean parametric shell (see "Upgraded parametric").

**COLMAP** (open source SfM + MVS) is the standard for poses and point clouds and is what most splat and NeRF tools
use underneath. It installs via Homebrew on Apple silicon, but there's no CUDA on Mac, so dense reconstruction is
CPU-only and slow. It's not needed while Object Capture's poses are available.

### Gaussian splatting

This currently gives the most photorealistic result from a phone video: reflections, soft light, clutter and plants
all look right from near the capture path. Mac trainers now exist (Brush via CorbeauSplat, msplat on Metal,
LuluSplat, RadianceKit, 3D Splat App). All are installs. On an 8 GB M1, training a room is feasible but slow.

- Separation: a raw splat is one cloud of millions of Gaussians, with no objects. Research methods (Gaussian
  Grouping, SAGA, OpenGaussian, SLAG) attach SAM-derived instance IDs so you can select, remove or recolor objects.
  Gaussian Grouping supports removal and "object location exchange" without retraining. These are research code
  that needs CUDA. Removing furniture leaves **holes** where nothing was ever seen (behind the sofa).
- Rendering: three.js viewers exist (Spark, SuperSplat, etc.). **Blender 5.3** (alpha now, release scheduled
  around 10 Nov 2026) imports and renders PLY/SPZ splats natively in EEVEE and Cycles, but has no export.
  **Blender 5.2.3 does not**; it needs an add-on such as BlendSplat.
- Splats don't relight. You can't change a lamp or the time of day convincingly, and new furniture placed into a
  splat won't cast or receive matching shadows easily.
- Verdict: excellent as a **"your room as it is" backdrop** or reference view; poor as the editable model on its
  own. Best paired with a parametric shell plus separate furniture.

Related: **Apple SHARP** (Dec 2025) makes a metric-scale Gaussian splat from **one photo** in under a second, with
Core ML and MLX conversions available. It looks great for small camera moves from that photo, but it's a research-only
license (apple-amlr) and a model download. **World Labs Marble** builds a navigable splat world from 2–8 photos of
one place; exports need a paid plan ($20+/mo; mesh GLB on Pro). It's generative, so it invents what it doesn't see.

### NeRF

NeRFs (Nerfstudio, Zip-NeRF) give similar quality to splats and can generalize slightly better far from the
capture path, but they're slow to train and render and in practice need an NVIDIA GPU. The field and the products
(Luma, Polycam, Scaniverse) have moved to splats. **Skip**; anything NeRF would give us, splats give us more cheaply.

### Apple RoomPlan (LiDAR, iOS)

RoomPlan guides a walk-around on a LiDAR iPhone/iPad and returns **parametric** data: walls, doors, windows,
openings, and furniture as labelled boxes (bed, sofa, table, chair, storage, fridge, fireplace, …), each with
dimensions and position. It exports USD/USDZ (as parametric shapes or mesh), supports multi-room merging
(`StructureBuilder`), and can substitute catalog models for the boxes on export. Mac Catalyst apps can read
and export results but can't scan.

- Realism: none on its own (grey boxes), but **dimensions are measured, not guessed**, and the result is already
  the shape of our spec: walls, openings, items as objects.
- Separation: native.
- Needs: Maeve's LiDAR iPhone, and a small iOS app (Xcode is installed; free personal signing works for her own
  phone), or a third-party app that exports RoomPlan USDZ, such as Polycam Space mode (LiDAR mesh plus floor plan; GLTF
  free, more formats on paid tiers).
- For Homebody this is the ideal **geometry source** on LiDAR phones (it matches "iPhone with LiDAR → use the
  depth data quietly"). It replaces Claude's guessed dimensions; photos still supply the look.

### Monocular depth + texture projection

A depth model (Apple Depth Pro: metric depth plus focal length; Depth Anything V2: relative, tiny, about 15 ms on M1
via Apple's official Core ML build) turns each photo into a 2.5D surface. With segmentation (Vision's subject
masks, or SAM 3 for text-prompted "sofa", "rug", "lamp"), each item becomes a textured depth layer, and the walls
and floor become planes fitted to depth. It looks photographic from near the original viewpoint and stretches when
the camera moves.
- Useful mostly to **measure** (fit wall planes, estimate item depth, check Claude's dimensions) and to project
  photo texture onto the parametric shell. Needs a Core ML model download (a few hundred MB for Depth Pro).

### Single-image-to-3D for furniture items

Meta **SAM 3D Objects** (Nov 2025, open weights, commercial use allowed under Meta's license; Meta reports about 80%
human preference over Hunyuan3D 2.0), Microsoft **TRELLIS 2**, Tencent **Hunyuan3D**: a cropped photo of the
user's armchair becomes a textured GLB in seconds to minutes. Hidden sides are invented, but plausibly. They
need an NVIDIA GPU locally, or a hosted API (fal, Replicate, Runware: cents per item).
- This is exactly the "Key implication" in the product vision: the same pipeline can turn retailer product
  photos into models.
- Separation: perfect, one object per item. Combined with the spec's size and position, the generated mesh is
  scaled into the item's bounding box.

### Matching detected furniture to real product models

Claude already writes a style and material description per item. Match it to a real product with a 3D model:
IKEA product pages expose GLB models (community scripts and extensions download them; the terms suggest personal
planning use, not redistribution), and retailers ship GLB/USDZ for AR. Dupe-style visual search could find the
product or a lookalike. When it hits, it's the most realistic result and directly shoppable (Transaction).
Licensing at catalog scale needs retailer or affiliate agreements, so this is a later-stage path.

### Our parametric approach, upgraded

Keep the spec → Blender pipeline (scale, separable items, relightable, editable), and pull the **look** from the
photos:
1. **Measured colors.** Sample wall, floor, ceiling and item colors from the photos at the pixels Claude points to,
   white-balanced against a neutral reference (ceiling or a white door). Today Claude estimates hex colors by eye.
   Vision/Core Image (`swift`) or Blender's `numpy` + `OpenImageIO` can average a masked region.
2. **Photo textures on surfaces.** Rectify a patch of floor, rug, wall or headboard from a photo (perspective-correct
   it with Vision's rectangle detection or four corners from Claude), make it tileable, and use it as the
   texture, replacing `oak_wood_planks` with *her* floor. For rugs, art and posters this alone is a big jump.
3. **Projection from known cameras.** If Object Capture returns poses for the room photos, put Blender cameras
   at those poses and **project each photo onto the parametric walls, floor and item boxes** (camera-projection
   UVs, then bake). This is the Simba and Linus "it looks like my room" effect, while geometry stays clean and
   separable. Unseen areas fall back to the matched material.
4. **Claude picks closer materials.** Give Claude a contact sheet of a larger Poly Haven set (hundreds of CC0
   materials, with thumbnails) and photo crops, and have it pick and tint per surface. The current `TEX` table has
   about 25 entries.
5. **Matched lighting.** Fit light to the photos: window direction and size from the spec, sun versus overcast from
   the photo (hard shadows or not), color temperature from white balance, and lamps on or off. Pick from a few
   Poly Haven HDRIs (overcast, clear, golden hour) instead of one sky, or use a panorama she shoots as the
   environment. (DiffusionLight estimates an HDR probe from one photo, but it's an SDXL-sized install, so it's for
   later.)
6. **Render-and-compare loop.** We already have "Fix it with Claude". Add a pixel check: render from the
   recovered photo poses and show Claude photo/render pairs at the *same viewpoint*, which makes the error
   obvious.

- Realism: medium-high, and up to "photographic" on surfaces that were photographed. Furniture shapes stay
  generic until per-item capture or matching replaces them.
- Everything here runs on this Mac today.

The pattern: **nobody ships a scanned room with editable, separated furniture.** Scanners (Polycam, Matterport, Luma)
give realistic but fused results. Editors (IKEA, Havenly, Modsy) give editable results built from parametric or
human-made models plus real products. The inspirations Maeve picked (Simba, Linus) are agent-built parametric models
with good materials and light, which is the route we're already on.

## 3. Recommended path

**Principle:** keep the parametric spec as the editable truth (scale, walls, separable items), and make it *look*
real by pulling surfaces, colors and light from the photos, then swapping generic furniture for real models of
her pieces one by one. Splats are an optional "as it is today" backdrop, never the editable model.

### Stage A: now, free, on this Mac, no installs
1. **Measured colors and photo textures** in `build_room.py`: sample colors from the photos with Claude-supplied
   regions; rectify and tile floor, rug and wall-art patches; use them in place of the generic textures.
   Tools: Blender's bundled `numpy` + `OpenImageIO`, or a `swift` script with Vision/Core Image.
2. **Bigger material library plus Claude picking:** add about 50–100 Poly Haven CC0 materials (already our
   source; downloading CC0 assets is the existing `npm run assets` step) and have Claude choose from thumbnails.
3. **Matched lighting:** a small HDRI set (overcast, clear, golden hour) chosen by Claude from the photos; sun and
   color temperature from the photos; lamps on or off as seen.
4. **Camera poses via Object Capture:** a `swift` script (`PhotogrammetrySession`, `.poses` and `.pointCloud`
   requests, `preview`/`reduced` detail to suit 8 GB) on the room photos or on frames from a video (`ffmpeg` is
   present). Use the poses to (a) project photos onto the shell in Blender, (b) render same-viewpoint
   comparisons for "Fix it with Claude", and (c) check spec dimensions against the point cloud.
5. **Per-item Object Capture** for one or two hero pieces (her sofa, a chair): a 40–80 photo orbit → USDZ →
   Blender (`pxr` is bundled) → scaled into the item's box. Use Vision subject masks to cut the background.
6. Measure every change with a fixed test room (`data/test-sample`) and side-by-side photo vs. render shots.

#### Stage A status (2026-10-29)
- **Done, in `blender/build_room.py`:**
  - Poly Haven PBR maps recolored to each surface's color, baked into the glb so the viewer matches the renders;
  - upholstered pieces built as subdivided, puffed cushions, plus L-sectionals;
  - shape cues from the read's notes (solid block, hairpin, wall-hung, black-framed, polished);
  - the sky lights the room through window light portals, with the HDRI's sun turned to come in through the main window;
  - 2700 K lamps, a 22–24 mm lens held level at 1.5 m, and per-shot auto exposure and white balance under AgX.
- **Done, `blender/photo_look.py`:** measured colors from Claude's `photo_regions` (or fallback bands), and the
  light kind (picks one of 5 HDRIs). Wired in `lib/scan/pipeline.ts` (look.json goes to Blender with `--look`).
- **Wired but unproven, `scripts/photo_poses.swift`:**
  - Object Capture poses run when there are 8 or more photos, and never block a build.
  - On a 30-frame synthetic walk-around it returned `processError`.
  - Two unrelated photos return `invalidImages`, as expected.
  - Using poses (projection, same-viewpoint renders) still needs a real overlapping capture.
- **Added after the critic's pass (same day):**
  - The builder now handles:
    - mono-pitch and gable ceilings with rafters and a beam;
    - slats and battens, a fireplace with surround, a live-edge floating shelf;
    - moulded shell chairs, tree-slice tables on hairpins, live-edge tables, cage pendants;
    - shag rugs, sheers, and L-sectionals joined from two items.
  - Built-ins are tagged `homebody_fixed`.
  - The backplate follows the read (`lakeside` for water), and eye views must see 3 or more pieces.
  - The exposure key comes from the photos.
  - `build.json` carries a `color_check` (every look.json color against its material, as a ΔE) and a `match`
    score per photo-matched render (`color_de`, `edges`).
  - Matched views come from `marks.json` or `spec.photo_marks`, solved in `photo_look.py`.
  - The spec gained optional `ceiling`, `outdoor_view`, `photo_marks` and `item.fixed`.
- **Not yet:**
  - photo textures (rectified patches);
  - per-wall colors (the accent wall is an item for now);
  - the frame color as a spec field;
  - per-photo exposure matching the photo's own grade.

### Stage B: needs Maeve's OK
- **Her LiDAR iPhone + RoomPlan** (if she has a Pro model): a tiny iOS app built with the installed Xcode, or
  Polycam Space mode to start. RoomPlan JSON/USDZ becomes the geometry source (measured walls, openings, item
  boxes) instead of Claude's estimates. Biggest single accuracy gain.
- **Core ML model downloads** (no package installs, but a download): Depth Pro or Depth Anything V2 for wall
  planes and item depth; Apple SHARP (research license) for single-photo splat previews.
- **Paid image-to-3D API** (SAM 3D Objects / TRELLIS / Hunyuan3D via fal or Replicate, about cents per item) to turn
  each detected furniture crop into a separable model. Same pipeline later serves catalog products.
- **Splat trainer install** (Brush/msplat/LuluSplat) plus **Blender 5.3** (around 10 Nov) for a photoreal
  "your room today" backdrop and comparison. Optional; slow on 8 GB.
- **Brew installs** (COLMAP, OpenCV) only if Object Capture poses prove unreliable on her photos.
- Product-model matching at scale (retailer/affiliate agreements) is a Transaction-stage decision.

### What to ask Maeve to capture (one room)
1. **Video walkthrough, 2–3 min, 4K/30, landscape**, phone at chest height. Walk the perimeter slowly with the
   camera facing into the room. Then do a second lap at about knee height, tilted up slightly, and a third lap high,
   tilted down. Lock exposure and focus (long-press AE/AF Lock), use the main 1× lens (not ultra-wide; keep the lens
   constant), turn off stabilization if possible, turn on the lights, and avoid direct sun in frame. We extract
   about 150–300 frames.
2. **Stills, about 20–30:** each corner from the opposite corner (4), each wall straight on (4), floor and ceiling
   (2), each doorway and window (with and without curtains), and **3 panoramas** from the center and two corners
   (as in Linus's demo).
3. **Hero furniture items (1–3 pieces):** 40–80 photos each, in three rings (low, middle, high), 70% overlap,
   the item filling the frame, plus one straight-on photo for texture. Skip glass and mirrored pieces.
4. **Close-ups for texture:** flat-on photos of the floor, a rug, the wall paint near a window, and any patterned
   fabric, each with a sheet of white printer paper in frame for white balance (and scale: 8.5×11 in).
5. **Two measurements:** one wall length and the ceiling height (each "I don't know" is fine).
6. If she has a LiDAR iPhone (and Stage B is OK'd): one RoomPlan or Polycam Space scan of the same room.
