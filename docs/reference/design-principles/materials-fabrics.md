# Materials and fabrics

Last updated: 2026-10-30. Part of `docs/reference/design-principles/` (see `README.md`).

Builds on `craft-standards.md` (the double-rub baseline, paint sheen, naming the wood and finish, patina) and

## 1. Upholstery durability

### The two abrasion tests
- **Wyzenbeek (ASTM D4157), US:** counts **double rubs** (one back-and-forth) with cotton duck, in one direction.
- **Martindale (ISO 12947 / ASTM D4966), Europe and UK:** counts **cycles** of a figure-eight (Lissajous) rub with
  wool cloth.
- **They don't convert.** Some sources suggest Martindale figures run about a third higher than Wyzenbeek for the same
  use, but there's no reliable factor. Compare like with like.

| Use | Wyzenbeek double rubs | Martindale cycles |
|---|---|---|
| Decorative (cushions, headboards, curtains) | 3,000–9,000 | < 10,000 |
| Light residential (occasional chairs, formal rooms) | 9,000–15,000 | 10,000–15,000 |
| General residential (most sofas) | **15,000+** | **15,000–25,000** |
| Heavy residential (daily family use, kids, pets) | **25,000–30,000+** | 25,000–40,000 |
| Commercial / contract | 30,000–50,000+ | 40,000+ |

- **Above about 50,000 double rubs, extra numbers don't buy much** at home. Fiber, weave and pilling matter more.
- **Also ask for pilling** (ASTM D3511/D4970, or ISO 12945-2; grade 4–5 is good) and **seam slippage**. A high rub count
  with loose weave still snags.
- **Cleaning codes (US):** W = water-based cleaner; S = solvent only; W/S = either; X = vacuum only. With kids or
  pets, prefer **W or W/S**, or removable, washable covers.

### Fibers and fabrics, by life

| Fabric | Durability | Kids | Pets (claws) | Sun | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Performance poly / olefin** (Crypton, Revolution, Sunbrella-type) | High | ✓✓ | ✓ | ✓ (if solution-dyed) | Bleach-cleanable versions exist; the safe default for families |
| **Solution-dyed acrylic** | High | ✓✓ | ✓ | ✓✓ | Pigment in the fiber; hundreds to 1,500+ hours of fade resistance |
| **Microfiber / microsuede** | High | ✓✓ | ✓✓ | ✓ | Tight, no loops; hair wipes off |
| **Tight-weave cotton or cotton-blend canvas** | Medium–high | ✓ | ✓ | – | Washable slipcovers; fades in sun |
| **Velvet (polyester or cotton)** | Medium–high (poly high) | ✓ | ✓ (no loops to catch) | ✗ (pile crushes and fades) | Poly velvet suits pets; crushes and marks |
| **Linen** | Medium | ✗ (wrinkles, stains) | ✗ | Fair, but natural dyes fade | Relaxed look; use blended or performance linen |
| **Wool** | High, self-healing | ✓ | Fair | Fair | Naturally stain-resistant and flame-retardant |
| **Bouclé (loop pile)** | Medium | Fair | **✗ (loops snag)** | – | Craft-standards: no bouclé where claws reach |
| **Chenille** | Medium | Fair | ✗ | – | Loops, pills |
| **Silk** | Low | ✗ | ✗ | **✗✗ (rots in sun)** | Accent cushions and curtains only, lined |
| **Leather: protected/pigmented** | High | ✓✓ | Fair (scratches show, but wipe clean) | Fair | Best "leather + kids" choice |
| **Leather: aniline / semi-aniline** | Medium–high | Fair | ✗ (scratches) | ✗ (fades, darkens) | Patina is the point (craft-standards) |
| **Bonded / "PU" / "genuine" leather** | **Low** | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | Peels in 1–3 years; never recommend as "leather" |

### Pattern, color and use
- **Mid-tones and small patterns hide wear** best (heathered, tweed, herringbone). Pure white and solid black show
  everything. With pets, choose a tone near the pet's hair color.
- **Kids:** performance fabric or washable covers; rounded corners on tables; no glass-top tables at toddler head
  height (about 18–30"); stable, anchored storage (tip-over straps).

### Sun and fade
- **Test:** AATCC 16 (or ISO 105-B02) lightfastness in hours. The Association for Contract Textiles guideline is
  **40 hours for indoor upholstery**; outdoor-grade fabrics run several hundred to 1,500+ hours.
- **Worst in sun:** silk (fades and rots), linen and cotton with natural dyes, saturated reds and blues, velvet pile,
  aniline leather.
- **Best:** solution-dyed acrylic or olefin, performance polyester, and light, neutral colors (fade shows less).
- **Rule:** in rooms with south or west windows (in the northern hemisphere) and pieces within the sun patch,
  specify fade-resistant fabric **or** UV-filtering sheers or film (craft-standards).

## 2. Wood

| Species | Color and undertone | Janka hardness (lbf) | Character | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **White oak** | Pale honey to tan; yellow-green-grey | ~1,360 | Straight grain, rays; the Scandinavian/Japandi default | Ammonia and tannin reactions with iron |
| **Red oak** | Pink-tan; **pink/red** | ~1,290 | Open, busy grain; very common in US floors | Pink undertone fights cool greys and green-whites |
| **Walnut** | Chocolate to violet-brown | ~1,010 | Mid-century signature; lightens in sun over years | Softer; dents |
| **Maple** | Creamy pale; yellow | ~1,450 | Fine, uniform | Yellows with age under oil finishes |
| **Cherry** | Pink-tan, **darkens to red-brown** | ~950 | Traditional, Shaker | Changes noticeably in the first year of light |
| **Ash** | Pale, straw | ~1,320 | Bold grain; bentwood | |
| **Beech** | Pale pink-tan | ~1,300 | Bentwood chairs | |
| **Teak** | Golden brown | ~1,070 | Oily, outdoor-safe; mid-century | Ethics and source; "teak finish" is often not teak |
| **Acacia** | Varied gold to dark brown, high contrast | ~1,700–2,300 | Budget hardwood, live-edge | Strong, busy grain dominates a room |
| **Mango** | Light to mid brown, streaky | ~1,100 | Budget, eco-ish | Uneven color |
| **Rubberwood** | Pale | ~980 | Budget "solid wood" (often stained walnut or oak color) | Stain hides species |
| **Pine** | Pale yellow, ambers with age | ~380–870 | Farmhouse, rustic | Soft, dents easily (a feature in rustic) |
| **Reclaimed / weathered** | Grey-brown | varies | Farmhouse, industrial | Check for finish and stability |

- **Construction:** solid wood > veneer over plywood (stable, fine for most) > veneer or foil over MDF or
  particleboard (budget; water swells it). "Solid wood" often means only the legs.
- **Tone count:** craft-standards caps it at 2–3 wood tones with matching undertones. Add: **make one wood dominant
  (60%+ of wood surface)**, and make the others clearly different (light vs dark), not "almost matching," which
  looks like a mistake. Repeat each wood at least twice.
- **Floors count as a wood.** Choose furniture woods relative to the floor: contrast in value (pale oak floor →
  walnut furniture, or vice versa), and match the undertone.

### Finishes
- **Oil / hardwax oil:** matte, natural, repairable by spot re-oiling; less resistant to water rings. Japandi,
  Scandinavian.
- **Lacquer (nitrocellulose, catalysed):** mid-sheen, durable; hard to spot-repair. Mid-century originals.
- **Polyurethane / conversion varnish:** most durable; can look plastic in gloss. Families.
- **Shellac / French polish:** high-gloss traditional; water and alcohol damage it.
- **Limed / cerused / whitewash:** white in the grain; coastal, French country.
- **Painted:** hides species; good for color.

## 3. Metals

| Metal | Temperature | Patina | Styles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unlacquered brass | Warm | Darkens, spots (the point) | Mid-century, traditional, art deco, Indian |
| Lacquered or PVD brass | Warm | Stays bright | Glam, transitional |
| Bronze / oil-rubbed bronze | Warm, dark | Wears to copper highlights | Traditional, Tuscan |
| Copper | Warm, pink | Deepens, verdigris | Rustic, farmhouse, boho |
| Brushed nickel | Cool–neutral | Stable | Transitional |
| Polished chrome | Cool | Stable | Modern, art deco, retro |
| Stainless steel | Cool | Stable | Modern, kitchens |
| Black steel / iron | Neutral | Stable (powder coat) or rusts (raw) | Industrial, farmhouse, minimal |

- **Mixing:** one dominant metal (about 2/3 of metal surfaces) plus at most one secondary; black counts as a
  neutral and mixes with any. Repeat each metal at least twice, in different zones.
- **Match the temperature to the scheme:** warm metals (brass, bronze) with warm neutrals; cool metals (chrome,
  nickel) with cool palettes; black anywhere.
- **Hardware unifies:** in one room, keep cabinet and furniture hardware within one family.

## 4. Stone and hard surfaces

| Material | Hardness and care | Look | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marble (Carrara, Calacatta) | Soft calcite; **etches** with acid (lemon, wine) and stains | Classic, luxe | Honed hides etching better than polished |
| Travertine | Soft, porous, pitted (filled or unfilled) | Warm, Mediterranean, 1970s | Fill and seal for tables |
| Limestone | Soft, porous | Quiet, French, warm | Stains |
| Granite | Hard, durable | Speckled, traditional | Can look dated in busy patterns |
| Quartzite | Hard, natural | Marble-like veining | Some "quartzite" sold is actually marble |
| Engineered quartz | Hard, non-porous | Uniform | Not heat-proof; can look flat |
| Soapstone | Soft, but inert | Dark grey, matte | Scratches (patinas) |
| Terrazzo | Durable | Mid-century, playful | |
| Concrete | Durable if sealed | Industrial, minimal | Cracks, stains unless sealed |
| Ceramic / zellige / terracotta tile | Durable | Handmade, Mediterranean, Moroccan | |

- With kids: avoid marble or glass coffee tables with sharp corners; prefer wood, round shapes, or upholstered ottomans.
- Stone reads as **cool and heavy**. Balance it with warm wood or textiles in the same view.

## 5. How materials pair

### Texture contrast
- **Every room needs at least three distinct textures**, and each anchor surface (sofa, bed, table) should sit
  beside a contrasting one: smooth leather on a nubby wool rug; a glossy lacquer tray on a matte linen ottoman;
  rough jute under a polished walnut table.
- **Texture scale:** fine (silk, lacquer, glass), medium (linen, oiled wood, leather), coarse (jute, bouclé, raw
  stone, chunky knit). Use all three scales; tonal and minimal schemes rely on texture to replace color.
- **Sheen ladder:** matte (plaster, linen) → satin (oiled wood, wool) → gloss (lacquer, brass, glass). Have one or
  two glossy notes per room to catch light; all-matte is dead and all-shiny is cheap.

### Warm and cool balance
- **Warm materials:** wood, leather, brass, wool, terracotta, cane, linen in oat or camel.
- **Cool materials:** marble, chrome, glass, concrete, steel, grey linen, blue-white paint.
- **Balance:** a room should be clearly one temperature (about 70% or more), with the other as a counterpoint repeated
  2–3 times. Examples: cool grey concrete loft + walnut + cognac leather + brass lamp; warm oak and oat room + a
  black steel lamp + marble side table + a blue-grey ceramic.

### Hard and soft
- Each zone mixes hard (wood, stone, metal) and soft (upholstery, rug, curtains). An all-hard room echoes (acoustics)
  and feels cold; an all-soft room feels shapeless. As a rough check, at least one soft element sits within reach of
  every seat, and at least one hard, structural element (a table, a frame or legs) is visible in every soft grouping.

### Style-family material kits (shortcuts)
- **Warm minimal / Japandi:** white oak + oat linen + plaster + paper + black accent; matte throughout.
- **Mid-century:** walnut or teak + tweed or wool + leather + brass + terrazzo; tapered legs.
- **Industrial:** black steel + reclaimed wood + leather + concrete + brick.
- **Coastal:** whitewashed or white oak + linen + jute + rattan + blue-grey ceramic.
- **Traditional:** cherry, mahogany or dark walnut + velvet and damask + brass + marble.
- **Indian thread:** block-printed cotton + brass + cane + carved dark wood + jaali.

## 6. Checkable material rules

1. Upholstery double rubs ≥ 15,000 (≥ 25,000–30,000 with kids, pets or daily heavy use).
2. With pets: no loop pile (bouclé, chenille, loose weaves) and no aniline leather on main seating.
3. Sun-exposed upholstery: solution-dyed, performance or light-tolerant fabric, or UV control at the window.
4. Wood tones ≤ 3, one dominant, undertones consistent; contrast with the floor's value.
5. Metals ≤ 2 plus black; one dominant; each repeated ≥ 2×.
6. ≥ 3 textures and at least one glossy and one matte surface per room.
7. The room's warm/cool balance is at least 70/30, with the minority repeated ≥ 2×.
8. No bonded leather described as "leather."
