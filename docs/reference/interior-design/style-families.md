# Style families

Last updated: 2026-10-27. Part of the Homebody interior-design reference (see `README.md`).

The major interior styles, written so the Taste Builder can (a) show each one as pictures, (b) recognize it in
people's own words, and (c) tell neighbors apart. Style boundaries are fuzzy and sources disagree on details; treat
this as a working taxonomy, not a standard.

**How to read each entry**
- **Traits:** palette · materials · shapes · patterns.
- **In their words:** what people say when they mean this style without knowing the name. Useful for mapping open
  answers into buckets.
- **Confused with:** the neighbor, and the tell that separates them.
- **Image set:** what to show. Every set uses the same four slots so families can be compared fairly:
  1. **Room:** a whole room, eye level, daylight.
  2. **Piece:** one signature furniture item on a plain background.
  3. **Surface:** a material close-up (texture, wood, fabric).
  4. **Vignette:** a styled corner (shelf, side table, bedside).

Keep lighting, crop and time of day consistent across families, so people react to the style, not the photo.

---

## The map

Two axes explain most of the space and work well as this-or-that questions:

```
                    MORE (layered, collected)
                              |
     eclectic / maximalist    |    grandmillennial
     bohemian                 |    traditional, French country
     Indian / South Asian     |    Art Deco, Mediterranean
                              |
 NEW / CLEAN-LINED -----------+------------ OLD / ORNAMENTED
                              |
     minimal / modern         |    transitional
     Japandi, Scandinavian    |    modern farmhouse
     warm minimal, organic    |    wabi-sabi (old in spirit)
     industrial, mid-century  |    coastal
                              |
                    LESS (calm, edited)
```

A third axis, **warm ↔ cool** (wood, earth, brass vs white, grey, chrome), splits the neighbors inside each corner.

## Modern and minimal family

### Mid-century modern
- **Traits:** warm earth tones (mustard, olive, teal, burnt orange) on neutral walls · teak, walnut, molded plywood,
  molded plastic, brass, leather · tapered legs, low and long profiles, organic-but-graphic curves, "floating"
  pieces · atomic and geometric prints, sunbursts.
- **In their words:** "retro", "Mad Men", "60s", "the Eames chair", "warm wood and clean lines", "my grandparents'
  credenza".
- **Confused with:** *Scandinavian* — mid-century uses darker teak/walnut, angular graphic forms, earth tones;
  Scandinavian uses pale birch/ash/oak, softer curves, white and pale grey. (They overlap: a Wegner chair is both.)
  *Modern/minimal* — mid-century is warmer, more wood, more color.
- **Image set:** walnut credenza with a sunburst mirror · lounge chair on tapered legs · teak grain close-up · a
  ceramic lamp and stacked books in mustard and olive.

### Scandinavian (incl. Copenhagen)
- **Traits:** white, pale grey, oat, with soft pastels (sage, dusty pink, pale blue) · pale woods (birch, ash, light
  oak, pine), wool, sheepskin, linen, paper lamps · simple, functional, soft-edged · minimal pattern, stripes,
  small checks.
- **Copenhagen variant:** the warmer, more personal Danish home: mismatched dining chairs, candles and lamps instead
  of the overhead "big light", paper lanterns, vintage Danish pieces, sometimes bold color or dark floors. The goal
  is **hygge** (cosiness), and minimalism is a tool for it, not a rule. Maeve cited this influence.
- **In their words:** "bright and airy", "cozy but clean", "IKEA but nicer", "hygge", "candles everywhere", "like a
  Copenhagen flat", "white walls and wood floors".
- **Confused with:** *Japandi* — Scandinavian is brighter and more social; Japandi is warmer, darker-accented, lower
  and more restrained. *Mid-century* (above). *Minimal* — Scandinavian is softer and cozier, with textiles.
- **Image set:** white room with light oak floor, sofa, wool throw, many lamps · a pale wood spindle chair · sheepskin
  and knit close-up · candle cluster on a windowsill. Show Copenhagen as its own set if a person bites (mismatched
  chairs, paper lantern, a colored wall).

### Japandi
- **Traits:** off-white, oatmeal, beige, warm grey, accented with charcoal, black, deep brown · light Nordic woods
  mixed with darker Japanese tones, bamboo, rattan, paper, undyed linen/cotton/wool, handmade ceramics · low,
  grounded furniture, clean lines, craftsmanship · almost no pattern; texture instead.
- **In their words:** "calm", "serene", "zen", "spa-like", "like a ryokan", "nothing extra", "natural and quiet".
- **Confused with:** *Scandinavian* — Japandi is lower, darker-contrasted, more austere. *Wabi-sabi* — wabi-sabi is a
  philosophy (beauty in imperfection) that Japandi borrows; a wabi-sabi room is rougher and more aged. *Warm
  minimal* — warm minimal has no Japanese reference and often more plaster and stone.
- **Image set:** low platform sofa, paper pendant, one branch in a vase · low oak bench · rough ceramic and linen
  close-up · tea tray on a low table.

### Minimal / modern (incl. contemporary)
- **Traits:** white, black, grey, sometimes one accent · lacquer, glass, steel, concrete, smooth stone · crisp
  geometry, flush surfaces, hidden storage · little or no pattern.
- **In their words:** "clean", "sleek", "no clutter", "modern", "gallery-like", "everything hidden".
- **Confused with:** *Contemporary* — "modern" technically means early-to-mid 20th century design; "contemporary"
  means whatever is current. People use them interchangeably; we shouldn't make them distinguish. *Warm minimal* —
  this one is cooler and harder.
- **Image set:** white room, low grey sofa, glass coffee table, a single artwork · a sculptural black chair ·
  polished concrete close-up · an empty-but-for-one-object console.

### Warm minimal
- **Traits:** oat, sand, taupe, clay, warm white · plaster and limewash walls, white or rift oak, travertine,
  linen, bouclé · restraint like minimalism, but **surfaces carry the interest**, not objects · tonal texture,
  no busy pattern.
- **In their words:** "minimal but not cold", "cozy minimalist", "neutral but warm", "quiet luxury", "beige in a
  good way", "plaster walls".
- **Confused with:** *Organic modern* — nearly the same; organic modern adds more curves and raw natural objects.
  *Japandi* — no Japanese references. *Minimal* — warmer, softer, matte.
- **Image set:** limewashed walls, oak floors, linen sofa · a travertine side table · limewash plaster close-up ·
  a stoneware vase on a plaster ledge.

### Organic / earthy (organic modern)
- **Traits:** warm neutrals plus terracotta, rust, olive, clay · raw and unfinished wood, stone, jute, rattan,
  boucle, clay, plants · curves, rounded edges, sculptural lamps · subtle texture, occasional mud-cloth or tonal
  stripes.
- **In their words:** "earthy", "natural", "grounded", "lots of plants", "desert", "texture", "warm and soft".
- **Confused with:** *Bohemian* — organic is edited; boho layers pattern and color freely. *Warm minimal* (above).
- **Image set:** curved sofa, jute rug, olive tree, clay lamp · a rounded wood stool · jute and bouclé close-up ·
  dried grasses in a terracotta pot.

### Wabi-sabi
- **Traits:** muted earth, ash, moss, charcoal · aged wood, rough plaster, stone, hand-thrown ceramics, linen with
  creases · irregular, handmade, asymmetrical, worn · no pattern; texture and patina.
- **In their words:** "imperfect", "handmade", "lived-in", "old and quiet", "nothing too perfect".
- **Confused with:** *Japandi* (more polished). *Rustic* (wabi-sabi is quieter, sparer).
- **Image set:** rough plaster wall, a worn wood bench, one crooked vessel · a hand-hewn stool · cracked glaze
  close-up · a single dried branch in shadow.

### Industrial
- **Traits:** grey, black, rust, brick red, cognac · exposed brick, concrete, black steel, reclaimed wood, worn
  leather, Edison bulbs · utilitarian, open loft volumes, metal frames · minimal pattern; maybe a vintage rug.
- **In their words:** "loft", "warehouse", "exposed brick", "Brooklyn", "raw", "metal and wood", "a bit gritty".
- **Confused with:** *Modern farmhouse* — both use reclaimed wood and black metal; farmhouse is whiter and
  homier. *Mid-century* — leather and metal overlap; industrial is rougher.
- **Image set:** brick wall, leather sofa, metal-framed windows · a steel-and-wood shelf · rusted steel and brick
  close-up · bare-bulb pendant over a reclaimed table.

## Classic family

### Traditional
- **Traits:** deep, rich colors (burgundy, navy, forest, gold) or classic creams · dark polished woods (mahogany,
  cherry), silk, damask, velvet, brass, crystal · symmetry, curved and carved legs, rolled arms, moldings ·
  florals, damask, plaids, stripes.
- **In their words:** "classic", "timeless", "elegant", "formal", "like a nice old hotel", "English country house".
- **Confused with:** *Transitional* — traditional is more ornate and formal, darker, more pattern.
  *Grandmillennial* — grandmillennial is traditional with humor and more color.
- **Image set:** symmetrical living room with a fireplace, paired lamps, a rolled-arm sofa · a wingback chair ·
  damask or velvet close-up · a brass lamp with pleated shade on a polished table.

### Transitional
- **Traits:** greige, cream, soft grey, taupe, navy accent · painted or medium woods, linen, performance fabrics,
  brushed nickel or brass · classic silhouettes simplified, comfortable, tailored · subtle pattern, tone-on-tone.
- **In their words:** "not too modern, not too traditional", "comfortable but put-together", "classic with a
  modern twist", "Restoration Hardware", "neutral and grown-up".
- **Confused with:** *Traditional* (simpler here). *Modern farmhouse* (no rustic elements). *Contemporary*
  (transitional keeps classic shapes). This is the most common "default" style; people often pick it because it
  feels safe, so confirm with a follow-up.
- **Image set:** slipcovered or tailored sofa, neutral rug, a pair of simple lamps · a clean-lined wingback ·
  linen and wool close-up · a tray with a candle and books.

### Modern farmhouse
- **Traits:** white, cream, black accents, wood tones, sage · shiplap, reclaimed and wire-brushed wood, black metal,
  galvanized steel, ceramic · exposed beams, barn doors, apron sinks, chunky tables · buffalo check, ticking stripe,
  simple florals.
- **In their words:** "farmhouse", "Joanna Gaines", "Fixer Upper", "rustic but clean", "cozy country", "shiplap".
- **Confused with:** *French country* — farmhouse is American, whiter, more black metal; French country is softer,
  more curved and patinated. *Industrial* (above). *Transitional* (no rustic).
- **Image set:** white walls, wood beams, slipcovered sofa · a trestle table with black windsor chairs · reclaimed
  wood close-up · a galvanized pitcher with greenery.

### French country
- **Traits:** soft cream, butter yellow, lavender, sage, faded blue · limestone, plaster, weathered oak, wrought
  iron, linen, toile · curved (cabriole) legs, painted and distressed finishes, rush seats · toile, gingham, small
  florals.
- **In their words:** "Provence", "French farmhouse", "romantic but relaxed", "rustic elegant", "like a French
  country kitchen", "antiques".
- **Confused with:** *Modern farmhouse* (above). *Mediterranean* — French country is softer and more neutral;
  Mediterranean adds terracotta, tile and saturated color. *Grandmillennial* (more prints and fuss).
- **Image set:** stone floor, a painted armoire, linen-slipcovered chairs · a Louis-style painted chair · toile
  close-up · copper pots or a bowl of lemons on a farm table.

### Mediterranean
- **Traits:** whitewash and sand, terracotta, ochre, olive, cobalt and sea blue · stucco/plaster, terracotta and
  encaustic tile, wrought iron, rough wood beams, rush, linen · arches, built-in plaster benches, chunky rustic
  pieces · patterned tile, simple stripes, Moorish geometrics.
- **In their words:** "Greek island", "Italian villa", "Spanish", "Mallorca", "holiday house", "sun-baked",
  "arches", "terracotta".
- **Confused with:** *French country* (above). *Organic / earthy* — overlapping palette; Mediterranean has tile,
  arches and blue.
- **Image set:** whitewashed arch, built-in plaster sofa, linen cushions · a rush-seat chair · patterned cement tile
  close-up · olive branch in a terracotta jug.

### Art Deco
- **Traits:** high contrast (black and gold, emerald, sapphire, blush, deep teal) · lacquer, velvet, marble,
  mirror, chrome, brass, exotic veneers · symmetry, streamlined curves, stepped forms, fan and sunburst shapes ·
  chevrons, zigzags, fans, geometric inlays.
- **In their words:** "glam", "Gatsby", "luxe", "jewel tones", "velvet", "a 1920s cocktail bar", "Wes Anderson
  hotel".
- **Confused with:** *Glam/Hollywood Regency* — close cousins; Deco is more geometric and architectural.
  *Mid-century* — both use brass and curves; Deco is shinier and more opulent.
- **Image set:** velvet curved sofa, fluted side table, brass lamp, black-and-white marble · a scalloped or
  channel-tufted chair · fluted brass or lacquer close-up · a coupe glass and a fan-shaped mirror.

### Grandmillennial
- **Traits:** blue and white, pink and green, chintz brights · dark woods, wicker and cane, needlepoint, pleated and
  ruffled lampshades, skirted tables, chinoiserie, ginger jars, collected china · antique-inspired shapes ·
  florals, chintz, toile, gingham, scalloped edges, mixed prints.
- **In their words:** "granny chic", "my grandmother's house but cool", "preppy", "florals", "chintz", "coastal
  Connecticut", "maximal but traditional".
- **Confused with:** *Traditional* (grandmillennial is younger, lighter, more playful). *Maximalist* (more
  traditional sources). Designers warn it tips easily from curated to cluttered.
- **Image set:** floral wallpaper, skirted table, rattan chair, blue-and-white jars · a cane-back chair · chintz
  and gingham close-up · a pleated lampshade over a stack of china plates.

### Coastal
- **Traits:** white, sand, soft blues, sea-glass green · whitewashed wood, linen, cotton, jute, rattan, driftwood ·
  relaxed, slipcovered, airy · stripes, subtle waves; avoid literal anchors.
- **Variants:** *Nautical* is bolder and literal (navy and red, rope, stripes, boat motifs). *Coastal grandmother*
  is a lifestyle look: white base, natural light, linen in soft neutrals, a Nancy-Meyers-kitchen ease.
- **In their words:** "beachy", "light and breezy", "Hamptons", "beach house", "relaxed".
- **Confused with:** *Scandinavian* (also light; coastal is bluer and more relaxed). *Mediterranean* (warmer,
  plaster, tile).
- **Image set:** slipcovered white sofa, pale blue pillows, jute rug, big windows · a rattan armchair · linen and
  whitewashed wood close-up · a glass jug of hydrangeas on a weathered table (not shells or anchors; avoid kitsch).

## Layered and global family

### Bohemian
- **Traits:** warm saturated colors (terracotta, mustard, plum, teal) · rattan, macramé, kilims, floor cushions,
  vintage wood, plants, global textiles · low seating, layered rugs, relaxed and unmatched · kilim, ikat, suzani,
  mud cloth, mixed prints.
- **In their words:** "boho", "free-spirited", "plants and rugs", "global", "Moroccan vibes", "relaxed and cozy",
  "collected on my travels".
- **Confused with:** *Eclectic* — boho throws out the rules with a free-spirited, global, textile-heavy look;
  eclectic bends them, mixing periods deliberately. *Organic* (boho is more colorful and patterned).
- **Image set:** layered rugs, rattan chair, trailing plants, floor cushions · a peacock or rattan chair · kilim
  close-up · a macramé hanger and candles.

### Eclectic / maximalist
- **Traits:** anything, held together by a thread (a repeated color, a scale, a material) · mixed eras and origins
  · mixed shapes · lots of pattern, pattern-on-pattern, gallery walls.
- **Split:** *Eclectic* is about **mixing** (it can even be fairly sparse); *maximalist* is about **density**
  (little negative space, more is more). Ask "a curated mix, or more is more?"
- **In their words:** "collected", "a bit of everything", "colorful", "personal", "I like a lot of styles", "no
  rules", "every object has a story".
- **Confused with:** *Bohemian* (above). *Grandmillennial* (traditional-sourced maximalism).
- **Image set:** a gallery wall over a vintage sofa with a modern lamp · an unexpected statement chair · two
  clashing-but-working prints close-up · a bookshelf with art, books and objects.

### Indian / South Asian influences
Maeve cited Indian influences. Treat this as an **influence layered onto a base style**, not one look; India is
many regional traditions (Rajasthani, Kerala, Chettinad, Mughal, Goan…). Contemporary Indian interiors ("Indo-
contemporary") pair one or two heritage focal points per room with plain walls, streamlined furniture and closed
storage.
- **Traits:** warm neutrals as a base with terracotta, mustard/haldi, indigo, deep green, marigold, pink accents ·
  carved teak and sheesham, cane, jaali screens (wood, stone, cut metal), antique or brushed brass, terracotta,
  handloom cotton and silk, dhurries · arches, low seating, swing (jhoola), carved panels · hand block print
  (Bagru, Sanganer), ikat (Pochampally), kantha, Mughal florals and paisley, mirror work.
- **In their words:** "my mum's house", "brass and block print", "colorful but calm", "Jaipur", "jaali", "warm
  wood and textiles", "festive", "I want my heritage in here but not a theme".
- **Confused with:** *Bohemian* — boho borrows these textiles loosely; a heritage-led room is more specific and
  meaningful. Be careful to treat it as personal heritage, not a costume.
- **Ways in for a non-Indian base:** a block-printed quilt or cushion on a Scandinavian sofa; an antique brass lamp
  or urli in a warm-minimal room; a jaali panel as a room divider; a dhurrie under a mid-century table.
- **Image set:** plain room with a carved teak bench, dhurrie, brass lamps, one jaali panel · a cane-and-teak
  chair · hand block print close-up · a brass diya and marigolds on a shelf.

## Common confusions, at a glance

| People say… | They might mean | Tell them apart by showing |
|---|---|---|
| "Modern" | Minimal, mid-century, contemporary, transitional, or warm minimal | Cool/sleek vs warm/wood; 1960s vs today |
| "Scandi" | Scandinavian, Japandi, or warm minimal | Bright and social vs low and serene vs plaster and stone |
| "Cozy" | Hygge Scandinavian, organic, boho, farmhouse, traditional | Pale and candlelit vs earthy vs layered vs rustic |
| "Neutral" | Warm minimal, transitional, coastal, Japandi | Warm vs cool neutrals; texture vs tailored |
| "Classic" | Traditional or transitional | Ornate vs simplified |
| "Rustic" | Farmhouse, French country, industrial, wabi-sabi | Shiplap vs limestone vs brick vs rough plaster |
| "Eclectic" | Eclectic, boho, maximalist, grandmillennial | Curated mix vs global textiles vs density vs chintz |
| "Glam" | Art Deco or Hollywood Regency | Geometry and lacquer vs mirrored and tufted |
| "Natural" | Organic, Japandi, wabi-sabi, coastal | Curves vs low lines vs patina vs breezy |

## Using this in the Taste Builder

- Never show style names first. Show room images; **name the style back afterwards** ("You keep picking warm wood
  and plaster. Designers call this warm minimalism.").
- Results are **blends**: "mostly Scandinavian, with mid-century warmth and a thread of Indian textiles".
- When someone lands between two neighbors, run a this-or-that pair from the confusions table.
- Influences from someone's story (Copenhagen, India) are a **layer** on top of the blend, expressed in two or three
  pieces, not a theme.
