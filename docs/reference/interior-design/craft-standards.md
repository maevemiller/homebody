# Craft standards for notes and briefs

Last updated: 2026-10-29. Part of the Homebody interior-design reference (see `README.md`).

The numbers and vocabulary the designer's notes and the brief use, so every line is a decision or a fact that
drives one. These are common residential rules of thumb, collected for Homebody's brief. They aren't yet sourced
line by line like the other reference files; check a number against a trade source before it drives a purchase.

## Space

- **Circulation:** main walkways 36" (91 cm); secondary paths 30" at least.
- **Sofa to coffee table:** 14–18" (35–45 cm). Coffee table about two thirds of the sofa's length.
- **Rug:** at least the front legs of every seat on it; 12–18" of floor showing at the walls.
- **Dining:** 24" (60 cm) of table edge per seat; 36" from table edge to wall behind a chair, 44" where people
  pass behind.
- **TV:** seat at about 1.5–2.5× the screen diagonal; screen center near seated eye height (about 42").
- **Focal point:** one per room (a window, a fireplace, a TV wall, a piece of art); seating faces or frames it.
- **Zoning:** in a room with two jobs (living and working, living and dining), a rug, a lamp or a shelf marks each
  zone.

## Light

- **Layers:** ambient (ceiling or several lamps), task (reading, desk, cooking), accent (art, shelves).
- **Color temperature:** 2700K for living and evening rooms; 3000K for task light; 4000K reads as office.
  Dimmers on ambient.
- **Windows, by the way they face** (northern hemisphere):
  - **South:** bright, even and warm most of the day. Fade-resistant fabrics (solution-dyed or performance
    weaves, nothing silk) and light-filtering sheers; it's the easiest light to live with.
  - **West:** hot, low sun after about 4pm, with glare. Sheers or solar shades, low-sheen finishes.
  - **North:** cool and even all day. Warmer whites and warmer lamps (2700K) to balance it.
  - **East:** bright, cool morning light that's gone by midday. Light-filtering shades if mornings matter;
    lamps carry the rest of the day.
  - Blackout lining wherever someone sleeps by day.

## Materials and finishes

- **Upholstery durability:** double rubs (Wyzenbeek) 15,000+ for residential, 30,000+ for heavy daily use, kids or
  pets. Tight weaves and performance fabrics with pets; no loop pile (bouclé) or loose linen where claws reach.
- **Paint sheen:** matte or eggshell on walls (hides flaws), satin in hard-use rooms, semi-gloss on trim and doors.
- **Wood:** name the species and the finish (white oak in matte hardwax oil, walnut in satin oil); keep to two or
  three wood tones, with matching undertones.
- **Metals:** unlacquered brass and semi-aniline leather patina, which is the point; lacquered or powder-coated
  finishes don't.

## Color

- **60-30-10:** 60% base (walls, large pieces), 30% secondary (upholstery, wood, rug), 10% accent (cushions, art,
  objects). Name the colors in each role.
- **Undertones:** warm whites (yellow or pink undertone) with warm woods; cool whites (blue or grey) with cool
  stone and steel.

## Lead times

- In-stock furniture ships in days to a few weeks; made-to-order upholstery and case goods take about 8–16 weeks.

## Writing it down

- A note is a fact and the decision it drives: "Cat on the sofa → tight-weave performance fabric, no loop pile".
- Style is named by reference (Mediterranean, mid-century), not by adjectives.
- No stock filler: "warm and inviting", "a sanctuary", "effortless", "curated", "gathered", "elevated",
  "cozy yet…", "timeless", "a space that tells your story", "the heart of the home". A mood word is used only when
  tied to its means: "warm = walnut + brass + 2700K".
