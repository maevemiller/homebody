# Homebody — Product Vision

Last updated: 2026-10-26

Homebody is an automated interior designer. It takes your real space, your existing furniture,
your taste, and your budget, and turns them into a harmonious, purchasable plan.

The current code (v0) is a form-based flow: intake → persona → functional info → style →
results, with a basic 3D room. This document describes where the product is headed.

---

## The four pieces (code names)

### 1. Space Capture
A **realistic 3D model of the user's real space**, plus the user's own furniture as separate objects.

- **Must be to scale and accurate**, so the system knows what fits when it proposes moving things.
- **Must be easy and seamless.**
- **Adaptive capture flow.** One experience, and the route underneath depends on what the user has:
  - Has a floor plan → use it for exact dimensions, then add photos or a short clip per room.
    This route especially serves fresh-start users who are moving and may not be in the space yet.
  - iPhone with LiDAR → use the depth data quietly in the background.
  - Otherwise (default) → a guided video walkthrough with live coaching.
  - Every route ends the same way: **"Here's what we found"** to confirm furniture → a quick
    close-up scan for any uncertain items → a one-tap scale check if still needed
    (e.g. "Is this door about 80 inches tall?").
- **Own furniture:** pulled out of the room capture automatically, plus a dedicated item scan
  where needed. Furniture may already be in the room, or not (e.g. when moving or in storage).

#### Space Capture: intake and inventory (2026-10-26)
Visual version, with the persona decision trees, a time-remaining preview, and an
inventory-sort mock: https://claude.ai/artifact/FpqhCTL1qbPxve6ntHxLGL

- **Two axes.** Persona (the 2×2) drives taste, service, and price. The *capture scenario*
  (scope, access, contents) drives Space Capture. They often go together but don't decide each
  other, so each persona has a typical path plus less common branches.
- **Intake, one question per screen (typeform-style):**
  1. Scope: one room / apartment / house. If more than one room, pick rooms, then capture each
     in turn. Start with one room.
  2. Access: can you be in the space? Yes → video walkthrough (+ LiDAR quietly). No → what do
     you have: floor plan / photos (e.g. a listing) / dimensions only. Dimensions only gives a
     box room with a visible low-accuracy label.
  3. Contents: empty (skip the inventory sort) / a few things / a lot.
  4. Bringing furniture from elsewhere? → item scan or product link, which goes straight to
     Keep, flexible.
  5. Already know something you want? → add by link or photo (Dupe-style match).
- **Progress (TurboTax-style):** a section rail (Your space → Capture → Inventory → Review)
  with check marks, plus "About N min left" recalculated after every answer. Per-step minutes
  are placeholders until the Space Capture test.
- **Inventory sort (after a room is captured), revised 2026-10-26:** one screen, not one item
  at a time.
  - A 3D dollhouse view of the room with a toggle: **Empty room / Everything / What stays**.
  - Below it, a list of every detected item. Multi-select from the list or by tapping items in
    the room, use "Select all to sort" and filter tabs, then tap a bucket.
  - The three buckets are color-coded in the 3D view:
    - **green, Keep here** (stays exactly where it is)
    - **yellow, Keep, move it** (staying, but goes somewhere else)
    - **red, Remove** (it's going)
  - Assigning yellow or red makes the item fade out of the room right away, so you see the
    change. Back undoes the last assignment.
  - "Done" keeps anything left unsorted where it is.
  - Yellow items, plus pieces scanned from elsewhere, go into a **"to place later" tray**. In
    Plug & Play, the user drags them into place or Homebody places them automatically.
  - Capture of furniture keeps furniture-level objects and ignores small clutter (as in the
    Simba loft example).
- **Persona typical paths:**
  - Showroom-style: floor plan + listing photos, empty space.
  - Considered first home: floor plan + photos, brings a few pieces, has a wishlist.
  - Quick refresh: video, full room, a fast sort.
  - Gatherer: video, full room, mostly Locked, has a wishlist.

#### Space Capture pipeline: working build (2026-10-26)
Real photos → Claude (Opus 5.5) reads the room into a to-scale spec (`lib/scan/spec.ts`) →
Blender 5.2 builds it (`blender/build_room.py`: walls with openings, Poly Haven CC0
materials, furniture generated by category, window and lamp lighting) → exports `room.glb` for
the browser viewer and renders a cutaway plus two eye-level Cycles stills. The viewer
(`/rooms/[id]`) has Empty / Everything / What stays and green/yellow/red sorting on the real
items. "Fix it with Claude" compares the renders with the photos and corrects the spec.
- Run it: `npm run assets` once, add `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` to `.env.local`, run `npm run dev`,
  then open `/scan`.
- Limits right now: it runs only on a local Mac (rooms are stored in `data/`, and Blender runs
  on the same machine). Furniture is generated from the room description, not copied from your
  actual pieces. It models one floor level.

### 2. Taste Builder
Getting to know the user's taste, the qualitative side of design, and their budget.

- An **AI voice interview**, not a form or quiz ("a quiz feels too lame").
- Output: a taste profile and budget that feed recommendations.

#### Taste Builder requirements (Maeve, 2026-10-27)
Start this only after Space Capture passes the design critic's "ready to present to Steve Jobs and
Jony Ive" bar.

**Use all the source material.** That means:
- the April 12 brainstorm: the fresh/refresh × standard/customized 2×2; the tradeoffs between
  speed, style, budget and customization; timeline; the persona names; the pricing concerns;
- story-driven personalization: where you're from and where you've lived (for example, Indian
  and Copenhagen influences), and "a personal story shows up first";
- the AI user profile principle;
- everything Space Capture already learned: the intake answers, `profile_signals` from the scan,
  and the keep/move/remove choices.

**Bucket people as early as possible.** Ask first the few questions that most narrow everything
after them, and skip anything already known (for example, from the scan or the intake).

**Order of questions, broad to specific.** Start with what applies to most people, then go
deeper by persona:
1. **Functional, for everyone:** how many people use the space, what it's used for, priorities
   for the space.
2. **Functional branches:** for example, kids? → how old? (young kids change materials,
   layout and safety).
3. **Lifestyle:** how they live, host, work and relax in the space.
4. **Style, broadly:** color, patterns, materials.
5. **Style, specifically:** narrower follow-ups that branch from their earlier answers.
6. **Personality and life story:** meaningful details to weave in subtly. For example, "my
   grandmother loved butterflies" becomes one small, considered nod, not a theme.

**Build the decision tree accurately.** Every branch must earn its time, because there's a real
tradeoff in how much time people invest up front.

**Optional depth.** Nobody is forced through all the content. There's a short core path, and
people can go deeper whenever they want, and come back later to add more.

**Voice-first and conversational** (Maeve's preference), keeping the one-question-at-a-time
calm of the capture flow. Maeve, 2026-10-27: it should feel like a live voice agent (Sierra, Decagon,
ElevenLabs), not a click-through. Voice is the default; typing is the alternative. The tree is the agent's map, but the
agent can take answers out of order and follow up. Plan and technical logistics: `docs/voice-plan.md`.

#### "What Homebody assumes about me" view (Maeve, 2026-10-27)
A toggle, available in this prototype only, that shows:
- the AI profile's current assumptions about the user (persona bucket, style, budget,
  lifestyle, meaningful details);
- why each assumption was made, citing the answer, scan signal or sorting choice it came from.

It must not ship in the final product. Gate it, for example as dev-only.

#### Visual style
Use the prototype's look: Newsreader for questions and the wordmark, Figtree for everything
else, near-white grounds, and a deep green accent (#2F5D50). This replaces the brown
Fume-inspired style.

### 3. Plug & Play
The space editor, built on top of the captured space.

- **Realistic visuals** (the Simba/Linus look) with **game-like interaction** (Club Penguin
  igloos, The Sims, Webkinz): playful and easy, not a CAD tool.
- A toolbar to move and edit things in the space.
- Plain-language instructions ("make this feel cozier").
- Hover or click an item → a **sidebar** with its price, alternatives, and a swap option.

### 4. Transaction
Buying. Overlaps with Plug & Play through the shared sidebar.

- A cart and item list in the sidebar.
- Buy the **whole place or room by room** (Webkinz-style).
- **Dupe-style alternatives:** "same look, different price" across retailers, with affiliate links.

### Shared logic
Recommendations = Space Capture + Taste Builder, and **vary by user type** (see the 2×2 below).

### Principle: the AI user profile (2026-10-26)
An AI model gets to know the user **from the first tap** and keeps building a profile through
every step.
- **Every answer is a signal.** For example, "a whole apartment" plus "I can't be there yet"
  suggests a fresh-start mover.
- **The capture itself is a signal.** It pulls out style cues (walnut mid-century console,
  bouclé chair), price level (mostly mid-range pieces), room facts (oak floors, south light,
  ceiling height) and lifestyle hints (lots of books, plants).
- **Inventory choices are signals.** Locking most things suggests a Gatherer; using "keep the
  rest" suggests someone who values speed.
- **It guesses the persona as early as possible** and keeps updating its confidence.
- **It hands off to Taste Builder**, so the voice interview opens with what it already knows
  ("I see you're keeping the walnut console…").
- **It's invisible to users.** The app stays a bit of a mystery; the profile is never shown to them.

Clickable prototype of Space Capture (one question per screen, with a progress bar and time
left; the "Behind the scenes" toggle shows the profile building):
https://claude.ai/artifact/2JamKqmqviqqzDqz3qCBHg

### Key implication
Realistic visuals mean **every product shown needs a realistic 3D model**, either from
retailers' AR models (glTF/USDZ) or generated from product photos (image-to-3D). That is
probably the same pipeline as the furniture scan in Space Capture.

## Inspiration

- **Simba** (@SimbasJungles, Oct 11 2026, https://x.com/SimbasJungles/status/2109095401273938072):
  modeled a whole Airbnb with Opus 5.5 from a few photos, the 2D floor plan, and Pinterest
  references, built in Blender and Unreal Engine 5. Reference for Space Capture.
- **Linus Ekenstam / Astra** (Sep 2026, https://x.com/LinusEkenstam/status/2097684798470816188):
  recreated his studio in Blender, accurate to the cm, from 5 photos and 3 panoramas in 11 minutes,
  then had Astra build a web viewer for the model. Reference for Space Capture.
- **Dupe.com** (https://dupe.com/): AI visual search for lookalike products across retailers,
  with prices and buy links; affiliate-funded; has an MCP server for agents. Reference for
  alternatives and Transaction.
- **Club Penguin, The Sims, Webkinz:** game-like decorating and buying room by room. Reference for
  the Plug & Play interaction (not the art style).

## Maeve's direction after the first demo (2026-10-27)

**North star:** democratize the experience of hiring a great interior designer: polished, presentable, warm,
engaging, thoughtful. When unsure about quality or direction, ask what a great designer would do with this client.

**Space Capture** captures the space as it is and what stays or goes, nothing more:
- "Building your room", never a named room type.
- Ask for a couple of measurements, each with "I don't know".
- No door-height scale check after the build.
- "Are these in your room?" comes later in the flow.
- No "something in mind" or wishlist step; that belongs in or after Taste Builder.
- Keep the Keep / Move sorting experience.
- Turn real photos and video into a photorealistic model, not the mock.

**Taste Builder** is an open conversation in structured sections:
1. **The function of the space**, then **aesthetics and taste**, then **the personal story**. People see the
   sections; they don't experience a list of questions.
2. **Open answers:** answers aren't limited to fixed choices. The model sorts people into buckets behind the scenes
   and follows where they "bite", shaping the conversation from that, the way a designer runs an intake interview.
3. **Visual choices for style questions:** palettes, rooms, materials and patterns are shown as pictures. "I'm not
   sure" opens an exploration (pick between images) that infers the preference.
4. **A live dossier:** a graphic view of the person's profile draws itself as they talk, like a hand-drawn
   whiteboard "day in my life" video. It replaces a plain question-and-answer list.
5. **Grounded in research:** how designers run intakes, design styles, palettes, and what designers ask. The research
   lives in `docs/reference/interior-design/`.

**Polish:** the UI must look like the work of a top-tier UX/UI designer; today it reads as rudimentary and AI-made.
Maeve will share references.

**Then Plug & Play**, the game-like editor, as time allows.
